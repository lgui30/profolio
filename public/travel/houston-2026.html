5085 Westheimer Rd. One of Texas's few Uniqlo locations. Hit the AIRism, linens, and anything on sale. The Galleria is massive — don't get sucked in for two hours.

15:30
Opera Gallery Houston — Monet & Masters
4444 Westheimer Rd, River Oaks District, two blocks from the Galleria. This gallery opened in March 2026 as Opera Gallery's first Texas location — and it launched with Monet's Les Bords de l'Epte à Giverny (1887), plus Picasso, Kusama, Chagall, and Kehinde Wiley. The collection rotates continuously from their global network of 15 galleries. Free and open to the public — no ticket needed, no hustle.
Free admission · River Oaks District is walkable, nice outdoor strip
17:00
Drive to Katy — Haidilao Hot Pot
23220 Grand Cir Blvd, Katy TX — about 35 minutes west on I-10. Worth the drive. Haidilao is the AYCE premium hot pot institution: tableside service that borders on theatrical, the noodle-pulling performance you've seen on every food reel, and a broth bar that actually slaps. Order the house-made tofu skin and the beef short rib. Budget ~2 hours at the table.
⚠ Reserve in advance on their app or walk-in and wait · waits on weekends can hit 60–90 min
21:30
Back to Houston — Montrose Night
Drive back downtown or land in Montrose for a nightcap. Axelrad beer garden is good if it's warm. The Menil neighborhood is beautiful at night — a rare walkable block in Houston.
Uber from Katy back if you're sharing a bottle at dinner

09:30
Hotel Breakfast / Coffee
AC Hotel has a solid lobby bar. Or walk a few blocks to Koffeteria or Boomtown Coffee in downtown for a better cup. No rush — the museum doesn't get crowded until after 11.
10:30
MFAH — Picasso · Klee · Matisse: Masterpieces from Museum Berggruen
1001 Bissonnet St. This exhibition opened May 20 — you'll be there in its first weekend. It's the U.S. debut of Heinz Berggruen's collection: 95+ paintings, watercolors, drawings, and sculptures covering Picasso and Klee's entire careers, Matisse's cutouts, Giacometti's elongated figures, and drawings by Cézanne and Braque. It runs through September, but opening week is the uncrowded window before word gets out.
Tickets required for this exhibition · buy online ahead · $25–30 pp estimated · museum opens 10 AM Sun
13:00
Lunch in Montrose
Montrose is a 10-min drive from the museum district. Indika for elevated Indian, Common Bond for sandwiches and excellent baked goods, or Hugo's if you want Mexican that actually means something. Take your pick based on how much time you spent inside Berggruen.
15:00
The Menil Collection (optional, free)
1533 Sul Ross St, a 5-minute walk from most of Montrose. One of the great free art museums in America — Renzo Piano building, Rothko Chapel next door, Cy Twombly Gallery across the street. If you only have 45 minutes, walk through the main building and spend it with the Surrealism rooms.
Free · closed Tuesdays · opens 11 AM
16:30
Bellaire Boulevard Drift
Drive south on Westheimer into Bellaire/Chinatown — this is Houston's second Chinatown, 99% Chinese-run, and nothing like the tourist version in other cities. Hit a boba shop, browse a Chinese grocery, watch the neighborhood breathe before dinner.
18:30
Gao's Kabob — Chinese Skewer / Chuanr
9888 Bellaire Blvd, Suite 118. This is the move for chuanr in Houston. Northeast Chinese-style BBQ skewers — lamb is the headliner, charcoal-grilled with cumin and dried chili, smoky and slightly funky in exactly the right way. Also get the garlic scallops, corn, and green beans. Beer towers available. It doubles as a music bar so it gets loud and fun late.
Open Sundays from noon · it runs until 2 AM · no reservation needed · cash-friendly
21:00
Stay in Bellaire or head back
The Bellaire strip stays alive late — boba shops open past midnight, and there are karaoke spots nearby if the mood shifts that direction. Or Uber back to the hotel and decompress.
Uber from Bellaire to downtown runs ~$18–25

Neighborhood Guide

Four Corners of the City

Houston is massive and car-dependent — knowing what each pocket is for saves you from wandering.

Montrose
Arts · Nightlife · Walkable (by Houston standards)
The closest thing Houston has to a Brooklyn vibe — independent galleries, gay bars, art-house cinemas, and restaurants at every price point. The streets around Westheimer between Shepherd and Dunlavy are the beating heart. This is your daytime wander zone and your late-night fallback.
Bellaire / Chinatown
Chinese Food · Night Markets · Strip Malls Worth It
Along Bellaire Blvd from Fondren to Beltway 8 — Houston's second (and arguably better) Chinatown. Mostly Chinese-run businesses with little tourist infrastructure, which is exactly the point. Every strip mall has three restaurants worth visiting. Gao's Kabob, boba shops, and late-night noodles all live here.
River Oaks District
Gallery Row · Upscale Shopping · See and Be Seen
The outdoor luxury retail strip on Westheimer near the Galleria — where Opera Gallery just opened. It's a two-block walk from Uniqlo and a very easy afternoon pairing. Grab a coffee at Velvet Taco or Pondicheri, then walk the gallery. The architecture is pleasant and the people-watching is good.
Museum District / Midtown
MFAH · Menil · Walkable Culture Zone
A rare corner of Houston where you can actually walk between things. MFAH, the Menil Collection, the Rothko Chapel, and Cy Twombly Gallery all sit within a 15-minute walk of each other. The surrounding streets have coffee shops and casual lunch spots. Budget a full morning here and you'll leave satisfied.

Practical Notes

Before You Leave the Hotel

Parking in Houston
The Galleria has free garage parking. Downtown near the AC Hotel is metered or use the ParkHouston app. Museum District has street parking and a free lot on Bissonnet. Bellaire: park in a strip mall lot — it's fine.
Uber vs. Driving
Drive to Katy for Haidilao — it's a freeway trip, easy to park. Uber for anything within the loop at night, especially after dinner and drinks in Bellaire. Surge pricing in Montrose on weekend nights is real — budget accordingly or walk a block before requesting.
Weather in Late May
Houston in late May: hot, humid, and occasionally stormy. Highs around 88–92°F, humidity makes it feel 95+. Dress in breathable fabrics. Afternoon thunderstorms are common and fast-moving — don't let them ruin your plans, just duck inside for 20 minutes.
Haidilao Reservations
This is the one thing that can crater the trip if you ignore it. Reserve on their app or by phone well in advance. Walk-in wait on a Saturday evening regularly exceeds 90 minutes. Book your Saturday dinner time before you leave Austin.
MFAH Tickets
The Berggruen exhibition requires separate timed-entry tickets. Buy online at mfah.org before you arrive — this is opening weekend and it will draw crowds. General admission ~$20–30 per person, check the site for current pricing.
The Bellaire Corridor
Bellaire Blvd from Fondren to Beltway 8 is a strip-mall corridor — not Instagram-pretty, but completely authentic. Don't be put off by the aesthetics. The food is better than anything in a curated food hall.
